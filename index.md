# Support

### Need help with *TXT? You're in the right place.

*TXT is pronounced "asterisk text."

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Getting Started

### Is there a guide in the app?

Yes. The first time you open *TXT, a short welcome shows the basics, and your library starts with a **Getting Started** note next to your pinned note: how to move around, the pinned note, the keyboard bar, Markdown and plain text, and where your files live. Delete it whenever you like; it won't come back.

### How do I make a new note?

*TXT opens to a new note, ready to type. You can also:

- Type in **Search or Create** at the top and press Return: if no note has that title, a new one starts with it, with the cursor ready on the line below the title.
- While searching, tap **+** (or press Option-Return on a keyboard) to always start a new note with exactly what you typed.
- Pull down from the top of a note and hold to start a new one.
- Use a widget, Control Center, Siri, the Share sheet, or your Apple Watch.

### What does it show when I open it?

That's up to you: **Settings → Open App To** can open a **New Note**, the **Last Opened Note**, your **Pinned Note**, or **Search**. It's ready the moment the app opens, even before iCloud Drive has finished loading. If you leave the app for a while and come back, it starts fresh there. **Reset After Leaving** sets how long "a while" is: Immediately, 1 Minute (the default), 5 Minutes, or 15 Minutes. A quick trip to another app leaves everything as it was.

### Why does a blank note show a \*?

That's the *TXT mark, standing in for your first word. It disappears as soon as you type.

### How do I move between notes?

Swipe left or right on a note, or tap the dots at the top. You'll feel a light tap as you move to another note, and a firmer bump at the first or last one. Notes are newest first, across all your folders, when *TXT opens; while you're using it, they keep their places, so editing a note (or a change from another device) doesn't shuffle the dots. Your pinned note has its own button.

### Is it like Notational Velocity?

Very much on purpose. One box does two jobs: type a few words to find a note, or press Return to make one with that title. Type the start of a note's title and *TXT fills in the rest — press Return to open it, or Backspace to keep just what you typed. On the Mac, the NV keys work too (see _Does *TXT have keyboard shortcuts?_).

### Searching

### How do I search?

Type in **Search or Create**. Results appear as you type, across titles, text, tags and folders. Capitals and accents don't matter.

- `milk bread` finds notes with both words, in any order.
- `"milk bread"` finds the exact phrase.
- `milk OR eggs`, `soup NOT tomato` (or `soup -tomato`), and `(milk OR eggs) bread`. Type AND, OR and NOT in capitals.
- `#work` or `tag:work` finds a tag and its subtags.
- `folder:Recipes` or `folder:"Work Projects"` searches a folder and its subfolders.

To find something inside the note you're reading, use the search button in the keyboard bar's Action Drawer.

### Can I find notes with Spotlight?

Yes. Your notes appear in Spotlight automatically. To hide them, turn off *TXT in your device's search settings (on iPhone and iPad, Settings ▸ Apps ▸ *TXT ▸ Search).

### Writing

### What can I write in a note?

Notes are Markdown, shown as rich text while you write. Headings, **bold**, *italic*, __underline__, checklists, bullet and numbered lists, tables, dividers, links and pictures all work. Use **Plain Text** in the ••• menu to see and edit the Markdown itself.

To type a literal `*` or `#` without it becoming formatting, put a backslash before it (`\*`).

### How do indents and dividers work?

- **Indent** and **Outdent** (in the keyboard bar's Action Drawer, under Lists) move a line in or out by a tab. One backspace removes an indent. How far a tab goes is set by **Tab Width** (see _Can I change how notes look?_). Indented lists become nested lists.
- **Divider** adds a line across the note. Choose its look in **Settings → Keyboard Actions → Divider**: **Line** (`---`), **Light Line** (`___`), or **Dotted** (`***`). All three are standard Markdown, so other apps show them as dividers too.

### How do I rename a note?

A note's title is its first line. To rename it, edit that line, or open **Note Info** and change **Title**. The file is renamed too, and links to the note keep working.

### How do tags and links between notes work?

- Type `#` and a word to tag a note, like `#work`. Use `/` for subtags: `#work/projects`.
- Type `[[` to link to another note; *TXT suggests titles as you type. Tap the link to open that note. Links keep working if the other note is renamed or moved.

### What is the pinned note?

One note you always come back to, like a shopping list. It has its own push-pin button above your notes (bright while you're in it), is marked with a 📌 in search, and shows on your Apple Watch. Its title is hidden while you write so it can't be changed by accident; rename it in **Note Info**. Choose it in **Settings → Pinned Note**, where you can also choose what happens to its text when you save it as a new note: keep it, clear it, or ask every time.

### Can I use templates?

Yes. In the ••• menu, **Templates → Create** saves the current note as a template, and **Templates → Insert** adds one to the note you're in. Templates are text files in a **Templates** folder beside your notes, so they sync and can be edited in Files or Finder.

### Can I do calculations in a note?

Yes. Add `#calc` to a note, or tap **Calculations** on the keyboard, and answers appear beside each line. Tap an answer to copy it.

- Words around numbers are ignored: `Hotel 4 nights × $185` → $740.
- Percentages: `15% of 80`, `15% off $80`, `$80 + 15%`, `$50 as % of $200`.
- Variables: `rate = $45`, then `rate × 30`. `total` adds up the lines above (back to a blank line or heading), `average` averages them, and `prev` uses the line above.
- Units: `5 km in miles`, `72°F in °C`, `2 GB in MB`, `90 min in hours`.
- Dates: `today + 3 weeks`, `next Friday`, `9am to 5:30pm`, `days until Dec 25`.
- Currencies: `$100 in euros`, using the European Central Bank's daily rates for about 30 major currencies. This needs the internet about once a day.
- `//` starts a comment.

### Can I change how notes look?

Yes. **Settings → Fonts & Themes** has fonts, text size, paper styles (Plain, Dots, Graph, Ruled), and:

- **Line Spacing**: Standard, or up to double (+20%, +40%, +60%, +80%, 2×).
- **Tab Width**: Half, Standard, x1.5, or Double. It's also how far **Indent** moves a line.
- **Editor Margins**: Standard (the full width), Small, Medium, or Large. Margins keep the text in a centered column on an iPad or a wide Mac window, and narrow along with the window; windows as narrow as an iPhone always use the full width.

The preview at the top shows each change before you save it. **Appearance**, **Accent Color**, and **App Icon** are in Settings too, and **Settings → Counts** chooses which counts (characters, words, lines) show above a note.

### Can I change the buttons above the keyboard?

Yes, in **Settings → Keyboard Actions**. Tap **Edit** to add or remove actions, drag to reorder them, move actions between groups, change the order of the groups, hide actions you never use, or delete symbols you've added. **On Keyboard** shows how many fit on your device. **Add Symbol…** makes a button that types a symbol or short text, like `@` or `->`. Tap **Checklist**, **Bullet List**, or **Divider** to choose its style. Everything else is in the Action Drawer on the left of the bar.

### Capturing

### Can I dictate a note?

Yes. Tap **Dictate** on the keyboard bar, or ask Siri, and *TXT types what you say. The audio isn't saved.

### Can I add a voice memo?

Yes. Tap the paperclip, then **Record Voice Memo** (or **Choose Audio** for a recording you already have). The memo appears in the note as a player. *TXT then asks whether to add a transcript of what's said: **Audio + Transcript** or **Audio Only**. Tap a memo's player for **Add Transcript Below** later. On the Mac, use **Note → Record Voice Memo…** or **Attach Audio…**.

You can also share a recording from the Voice Memos app: **Share → *TXT**.

### Why didn't my transcript work?

Transcripts use Apple's speech recognition. Check that Speech Recognition is allowed for *TXT in your device's Settings. If you've just started using it, your device may still be downloading the speech model for your language — try again in a few minutes. Recordings with no speech, or only music, have nothing to transcribe.

### Can I get the text out of a photo or scan?

Yes, with **Extract Text** on the keyboard bar. Scan text straight into the note with the camera, or take a photo, scan a document, or choose an image or file, then keep **Image + Text** or **Text Only**. You can also tap a picture that's already in a note for **Add Text Below** or **Replace with Text**. It all happens on your device, and handwriting works too.

### Can I add notes from other apps?

Yes. Use the Share sheet in another app and choose *TXT to save text, links, images, or recordings, and pick which folder they go in. Documents like PDFs can't be shared this way; bring them in with **Settings → Storage → Import Files or Folder…** instead.

### Can I drag things into a note?

Yes, on iPhone and iPad. Drag text, links, photos, recordings (from Voice Memos or Files), or files into a note, and they're added where you drop them, just like adding them with the paperclip. Pasting a copied photo or file works the same way. On an iPad, Split View or Slide Over makes this easiest.

### Importing and Exporting

### What can I import?

Text, Markdown, MultiMarkdown, TaskPaper, Word (.docx), RTF, PDF (text and scans), web pages, photos (with their text), recordings (with a transcript), Evernote exports (.enex), TextBundle files from apps like Bear and Ulysses, Obsidian vaults, folders, and zip files.

Use **Settings → Storage → Import Files or Folder…**, or drop files into the *TXT folder in Files or Finder — into **Import**, into **Notes**, or anywhere in the folder. They're picked up automatically.

### Where do imported notes go?

Into **Notes → Imported**. A folder, zip, or export gets its own folder there, keeping its structure. From there, move notes wherever you like. The original files are kept untouched in **Originals**, and the **Import** folder empties itself once it's done.

### Something didn't import. What now?

Files that couldn't be imported are moved to the **Not Imported** folder, and **Settings → Storage → Errors** lists each one with the reason. **Show in Files** (or **Show in Finder** on the Mac) takes you to it. **Clear Errors** clears the list but doesn't delete any files. Importing the exact same file again is skipped and listed as "Already imported."

### How do I export a note?

Open the ••• menu and choose **Export**: Rich Text, Plain Text, PDF, HTML, or TaskPaper. If the note has attachments, you can include them in a zip.

### Notes, Files, and iCloud

### Where are my notes stored?

As plain .txt files in your iCloud Drive, in the **asteriskTXT** folder, so they sync to your other devices. If iCloud isn't available, they're kept on the device in the Files app, under **On My iPhone → *TXT → asteriskTXT Library** (or On My iPad). Your notes are ordinary files: you can open them in any app, back them up, or take them anywhere.

To see a note's file, open **Note Info** and choose **Show in Files**. On the Mac, choose **Show in Finder** or **Open With**, or press ⌘⇧O to open it in your default text editor. Changes you make in another app come back to *TXT.

### Can I organize notes into folders?

Yes. Open **Library** from the ••• menu to browse, create, move, and delete folders and notes. **Settings → Storage → New Notes In** chooses where new notes go. You can also move a note from **Note Info → Move To**, or move files in Files or Finder.

### I deleted a note by mistake

Deleted notes stay in **Library → Recently Deleted** for 30 days. Tap one to restore it: *TXT asks first, then puts it back where it was (bringing back its folder too) and opens it.

### Does *TXT require an account?

No. There's no account to make. Your notes live in your own iCloud Drive or on your device.

### Apple Watch, Widgets, Siri, and the Mac

### Is there an Apple Watch app?

Yes. Tap the big **+** to dictate, scribble, or type a note. Choose where it goes in **iPhone Settings → Apple Watch → New Notes Go**: **Create New**, **Prepend to Pinned** (at the top of your pinned note, under its title), **Append to Pinned** (at the end), or **Ask Every Time**. Your pinned note shows below the **+**, and you can tick its checklist items. Watch complications open *TXT to the **+** screen.

### Why does a note from my Watch take a moment to appear?

The Watch can't reach iCloud Drive itself, so it sends what you write to your iPhone, which saves it. When your iPhone is nearby that's almost instant. When it isn't, the Watch holds on to it and sends it as soon as they reconnect — nothing is lost.

### How do I use widgets and Control Center?

On the Home Screen, add **New Note**, **Recent Notes**, or **Selected Note** (a note you choose, kept close by). On the Lock Screen, add **Dictate Note** or **Pinned Note** — the Pinned Note widget opens it without showing its contents. In Control Center, add **Open *TXT** or **Dictate Note**.

### Does *TXT work with Siri and Shortcuts?

Yes. Ask Siri to "create a note in asterisk text," "add to my pinned note in asterisk text," or "find notes in asterisk text." The Shortcuts app has actions to create notes (with text, links, or files), add to your pinned note, find notes, dictate, and open *TXT.

### Does *TXT have keyboard shortcuts?

On the Mac:

- **⌘N** new note
- **⌘L** go to search, ready to type over
- **Esc** clear search, then close it
- **Tab** open the highlighted result
- **⌘J / ⌘K** next / previous note
- **⌘⇧O** open the note's file in your default text editor
- **⌘⌥⌫** delete the note
- **⌘⇧M** switch between rich text and plain text

On the Mac you can also set a keyboard shortcut to show the *TXT window from anywhere, show it in the menu bar, hide it from the Dock, and open it at login, all in Settings. A function key on its own (like F6) works as the shortcut; if your keyboard's top row controls brightness and volume, hold **fn** while pressing it.

### How does the Mac menu bar icon work?

Click it to show or hide the *TXT window. Right-click (or Control-click) it for **Open Window**, **Open Settings**, and **Quit \*TXT**. Closing the window only puts it away: the menu bar icon, your shortcut, or the Dock brings back the same window, with your note just as you left it.
